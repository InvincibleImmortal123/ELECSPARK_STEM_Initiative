// 01   ABOUT THE SERIES

Science you can touch, not just read about.

ElecSpark Initiative is a free, student‑led STEM series where families explore electricity, energy, magnetism, robotics, and optics through live exhibits they operate themselves — crank a generator, float a magnet, steer a robot. Every station turns curiosity into real engineering thinking.

// Built to spark curiosity, creativity & engineering thinking.

  • How electricity is generated and transferred
  • How robots sense & respond
  • How optical principles work
  • How action & reaction forces work
// 02   EXHIBIT MAP

Seven stations. Real circuits. Real science.

Walk the full loop in an afternoon, or camp out at the one that grabs your kid the most — every station runs all day, every fair.

1
Station 01

Power Generation

How chemical and mechanical energy convert into electrical energy — three ways to make your own power.

1.1

Fruit Battery Experiment

Chemical energyElectrochemistryVoltage

Activity: Visitors connect different fruits or liquids and compare which produces the strongest electrical output.

1.2

Salt Water Electricity

ConductorsIons in solutionResistance

Activity: Visitors test different solutions and observe how conductivity changes.

1.3

Hand-Crank Generator

Mechanical conversionEM inductionRenewables

Activity: Visitors turn a hand crank to generate electricity and light an LED.

2
Station 02

Wireless Power Transmission

How electricity can travel without a single physical wire.

EM inductionMagnetic fieldsEnergy transfer

Activity: Visitors place a receiver coil near a transmitter coil and watch an LED light up with no direct connection.

3
Station 03

Electromagnetic Field & Force

The relationship between electricity and magnetism, made visible.

3.1

Lorentz Force Demonstration

Magnetic forceElectric currentElectromagnetism

Activity: Visitors observe how a magnetic field influences a moving charged particle.

3.2

Magnetic Levitation

Magnetic fieldsRepulsion & attractionForce balance

Activity: Visitors watch an object float, suspended by invisible magnetic force.

4
Station 04

High-Voltage Electrical Phenomena

A plasma ball brings high-voltage physics to your fingertips.

Electric fieldsIonized gasesPlasma physics

Activity: Visitors touch the glass surface and watch electrical energy interact with the plasma inside.

5
Station 05

Line-Following Robot

How robots sense their surroundings and make decisions.

SensorsControl systemsRobotics

Activity: Visitors watch a robot follow a path using its onboard sensors.

6
Station 06

Optics & Vision

How light, reflection, and perception shape what we see.

6.1

3D Hologram Display

Light reflectionOptical illusionVisualization

Activity: Visitors watch a floating 3D image created entirely by light reflection.

6.2

Visual Illusions

Visual perceptionDepth & pattern cues

Activity: Visitors compare what they expect to see with what's actually on the page — and discover how the two can differ.

7
Station 07

Force & Atmospheric Pressure

The invisible forces of air pressure and momentum, felt firsthand.

7.1

Atmospheric Pressure Demonstration

Atmospheric pressurePartial vacuum

Activity: Visitors watch atmospheric pressure hold a plate in place against a partial vacuum inside a glass.

7.2

Newton’s Cradle

MomentumImpulse

Activity: Visitors pull back one end ball and release it. As it swings down, it transfers momentum through the resting balls, sending the opposite ball swinging out — a hands-on look at Newton’s laws of motion.

7.3

Gas Reaction Force

ForceNewton’s 3rd law

Activity: Visitors blow air through a central pipe. As gas escapes through side pipes, it pushes back, causing the cup to spin — action and reaction, made visible.
